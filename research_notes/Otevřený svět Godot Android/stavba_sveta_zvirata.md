# Building the world from data and rules, and animating animals and NPCs (Godot 4, Android)

Scope: (a) building an open world from data and rules, with automated checks so the same errors stop coming back; (b) cheap, believable animal and NPC animation in Godot 4 on mobile. Research date: 29 Oct 2026. Note: the GDC Vault videos and the large Horizon PDF could not be fetched (paywall and size limit). Details from those talks come only from the abstracts and summaries, and are marked as such.

## 1. How AAA studios build worlds from rules (Far Cry 5, Horizon Zero Dawn, Ghost of Tsushima, KCD)

### Takeaway
All three AAA examples follow the same pattern. Artists write **placement rules** (a graph editor, Houdini tools, or a GPU language for rules), not individual objects. The world is **generated deterministically** from input maps, so a big change (moving a road or a mountain) is just a data edit followed by a regeneration. No public source describes KCD2's procedural placement.

### Cited Findings
- **Horizon Zero Dawn (GDC 2017, Jaap van Muijden):** artists define placement rules in a graph editor, and GPU algorithms build the dense world around the player as they move. The system places sounds, effects, wildlife and gameplay elements as well as rocks and trees. — [Guerrilla – GPU-based procedural placement](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/gpu-based-procedural-placement-in-horizon-zero-dawn)
- **Horizon:** the abstract gives examples of large edits made through data: painting in a tree line, redirecting a road, moving a mountain, turning a desert into a tropical swamp. — [Guerrilla](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/gpu-based-procedural-placement-in-horizon-zero-dawn)
- **Horizon slides** (per the search summary of the official PDF): 500+ asset types, about 250 placed per frame while the player moves. The stated design goals are "data driven, deterministic and locally stable", and ecotopes decide which asset types appear. The team started on the CPU and moved to GPU density-based placement. — [Slides PDF](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/GDC2017_VanMuijden_GPUBasedProceduralPlacementInHorizonZeroDawn.pdf) (not read directly: the file exceeded the size limit)
- **Horizon:** three people made the nature assets for the whole game. Various map types are combined in the Decima engine to get the final result. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/the-procedural-nature-of-the-horizon-zero-dawn)
- **Far Cry 5 (GDC 2018 / Houdini HIVE 2018, Étienne Carrier, Ubisoft Montreal):** Houdini tools generated biomes, textured the terrain, built freshwater networks (rivers) and generated cliff rocks. The talk's framing: "how do you fill 100 km² of wilderness with a terrain that changes every day?" Filling the space is easy; making it look natural is hard. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/houdini-procedural-world-generation-of-far-cry-5/), [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025557/Procedural-World-Generation-of-Far), [Vimeo recording](https://vimeo.com/273986776)
- **Far Cry 5:** every plant species has its own environment type in which it grows (vegetation by habitat). — [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2018/03/22/the-procedural-world-generation-of-far-cry-5/)
- **Ghost of Tsushima (GDC 2021, Matthew Pohlmann, "Samurai Landscapes"):** in-engine world-building tools, plus a **functional programming language that runs as bytecode on the GPU**, in which artists write texture and placement rules for generated data. The game needed millions of instances. — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027352/Samurai-Landscapes-Building-and-Rendering)
- **Ghost of Tsushima, grass (GDC 2021, Eric Wohllaib):** grass is generated procedurally over large areas, and blades are bent procedurally according to their height. — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027214/Advanced-Graphics-Summit-Procedural-Grass); a Godot reimplementation of the ideas: [2Retr0/GodotGrass](https://github.com/2Retr0/GodotGrass)
- **KCD / KCD2:** KCD1 shipped mod tools as a modified CRYENGINE Sandbox with a dozen tools, documented on the Nexus Mods wiki. KCD2 has had official mod support since patch 1.2 (March 2025, Steam Workshop), covering "over 300 elements" (UI, combat, RPG, AI). Nothing was found on procedural vegetation or level editing. — [CRYENGINE news](https://www.cryengine.com/news/view/official-modding-tools-released-for-kingdom-come-deliverance), [VGC](https://videogameschronicle.com/news/kingdom-come-deliverance-2-receives-massive-update-with-over-1000-fixes-and-mod-support)
- The generic CRYENGINE vegetation workflow (vegetation categories and objects, placed by painting in the level) is documented by Crytek, not by Warhorse. — [CRYENGINE Vegetation Creation](https://www.cryengine.com/docs/static/engines/cryengine-3/categories/1114113/pages/1048864)

### Inferences
- Our model (JSON rules → Blender export) matches the industry principle "rules, not objects". The difference is iteration speed. Far Cry 5 regenerated terrain daily and Horizon generates the world at runtime, whereas our full export takes about 23 minutes. That points towards an export split into parts with caching, plus a fast preview (a top-down map) before the full export.
- "Locally stable" (Horizon) is the key property against regressions: changing one rule or one region must not move objects anywhere else. In practice this means a seed per object derived from its position or ID (a hash of the coordinates), not one global random generator whose sequence shifts whenever an item is added.
- Exclusion zones around roads, buildings and water are typically implemented as input masks (distance-to-road maps and similar) that the density rule multiplies by. This is an inference from "various map types combined" (Horizon) and the Far Cry 5 river networks; the details are in the paywalled talks.

### Gaps
- The exact Horizon footprint/exclusion and density-map mechanics (in the PDF, which could not be read: 10 MB limit). Recommended: download the PDF and read the slides about footprints and ecotopes.
- The Far Cry 5 and Ghost of Tsushima talks themselves (GDC Vault paywall; the Vimeo recording of the Far Cry 5 talk is free).
- Nothing public was found on KCD2 world building.

## 2. Automated world validation and protection against recurring errors

### Takeaway
Only one primary studio source was found that describes systematic world validation directly: **Assassin's Creed Origins (GDC 2018)**, with daily automated tests and reports on world data, a visual status display for the team, and regression tracking. Visual regression testing (screenshots from fixed cameras compared against a baseline with a tolerance) is general practice, but no public studio source describes specific checks such as "floating object" or "road over river".

### Cited Findings
- **Assassin's Creed Origins: Monitoring and Validation of World Design Data** (Nicholas Routhier, Ubisoft Montreal, GDC 2018): an open world is too complex to validate by hand, especially where hand-placed and procedural data (AI, art, design) depend on each other. The team ran **daily automated tests and reports**, showed the world's state visually to the world-building team, and the pipeline surfaced errors and **tracked regressions**. — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025452/-Assassin-s-Creed-Origins)
- Sea of Thieves (Rare, GDC 2019) explained why the team chose automated testing for the game. — [GDC news](https://gdconf.com/news/sea-thieves-devs-share-automated-testing-tips-gdc-2019)
- Ubisoft tested The Division with AI-controlled players (automated playthroughs). — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/gdc-using-ai-controlled-players-to-test-the-division/)
- Asset validation as a team tool (an asset-health dashboard, Voyagers of Nera, Virtuos). — [GDC schedule](https://schedule.gdconf.com/session/smooth-sailing-using-asset-health-metrics-to-optimize-voyagers-of-nera-presented-by-virtuos/917459)
- Visual regression in games: capture screenshots in a known state and compare them with a baseline; the state must be **exactly the same** (a hook loads the level and fixes the camera); exact pixel comparison gives false positives (antialiasing, GPU), so a threshold is needed and the build fails only above it. — [abratabia – visual testing](https://abratabia.com/game-testing/visual-testing.php) (secondary source, not a studio)
- Example of a published level-validation checklist (BeamNG level-creation docs, "Testing and validation"). — [BeamNG docs](https://docs.beamng.com/modding/levels/level_creation/section9/)

### Inferences
- The Míša/rádce rule "every error a person finds becomes a new check in the test" is exactly the AC Origins model (daily tests plus regression tracking). Concrete recommendation: keep a table of errors, where each row is an ID, a description, the check function, and the first version in which it was fixed. The test fails if any row regresses, and the report shows it visually (a map with red dots, i.e. our `build/mapa_stavby.png`).
- The geometric checks to put in `test_smyslu.py` (inference; no studio publishes the list): (1) floating object = height of the object's base minus terrain height at its footprint points > tolerance, or a raycast down from the corners finds no ground; (2) object in water = footprint ∩ water polygon/mask; (3) road over a river without a bridge = intersection of the road polyline with the river polyline, where no crossing record exists at that point; (4) building on a slope = maximum terrain gradient under the footprint > the limit from `pasma`; (5) vegetation on a road = point in the road polygon widened by the zone width.
- Screenshot regression: a fixed set of N cameras (positions in JSON), `--write-movie`, or one frame per camera after a fixed number of frames; compare with the last approved baseline (perceptual difference/SSIM, threshold). Images above the threshold go to a person, and an approved image becomes the new baseline. Deterministic generation (seeds by position) is a precondition, otherwise every image differs.
- Errors come back mainly when (a) generation is not locally stable, (b) a fix was made by hand in the output (a scene) and not in the rules, so it disappears on regeneration, or (c) the check was never written. This is consistent with the "no patching" rule.

### Gaps
- No public studio talk describing the specific checks "floating object / object in water / road over river" was found. Recommended: the AC Origins talk on GDC Vault (paywall) for detail.
- No Godot-specific framework for screenshot regression of a 3D world was found (search was limited).

## 3. Blender → Godot 4: import pipeline and its pitfalls

### Takeaway
The main risks when importing large scenes: **automatic LOD generation** (meshoptimizer) can break some meshes, especially skinned ones; it should be turned off per mesh where geometry is thin or fragmented, or replaced with hand-made LODs through visibility ranges. On Mobile/Compatibility, visibility-range fading does not work (it switches instantly).

### Cited Findings
- **Generate LODs** on import creates lower-detail variants; it is not worth it for every mesh. Turning it off reduces file size and import time. — [Godot docs – Import configuration](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/importing_3d_scenes/import_configuration.html)
- Automatic LODs use the **meshoptimizer** library; "may occasionally introduce rendering issues (especially in skinned meshes)". For a broken mesh, disable LODs in the Import dock or Advanced Import Settings. — [Godot docs – Mesh LOD](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/mesh_lod.html)
- `Rendering > Mesh LOD > LOD Change > Threshold Pixels` defaults to 1 px (perceptually lossless); a higher value switches sooner. Per object: `LOD Bias` on a GeometryInstance3D (>1 = switch later, higher quality). — [Godot docs – Mesh LOD](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/mesh_lod.html)
- For hand-made LODs the docs recommend **visibility ranges (HLOD)** instead of automatic LODs. — [Godot docs – Mesh LOD](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/mesh_lod.html)
- Visibility ranges: the Begin/End Margin is either hysteresis or an alpha fade, depending on Fade Mode. "Disabled" is the fastest (no transparency); "Self" and "Dependencies" force transparent rendering during the transition. Dithering is possible through materials with distance fade = Object Dither. Per the class reference (a translated snippet), Self/Dependencies work only in Forward+, while on Mobile/Compatibility they behave like Disabled. — [Godot docs – Visibility ranges](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.2/tutorials/3d/visibility_ranges.html), [GeometryInstance3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/zh-cn/4.3/classes/class_geometryinstance3d.html)
- **Create Shadow Meshes** welds vertices for shadows (less bandwidth with no quality loss). **Apply Root Scale** bakes the scale into meshes and animations and keeps the root at 1,1,1. **Import script** (`EditorScenePostImport`, `@tool`, `_post_import(scene)` must return the scene) runs after every import. Godot never overwrites the source file; local edits go into an inherited scene. — [Godot docs – Import configuration](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/importing_3d_scenes/import_configuration.html)
- Import hints = suffixes in object names in Blender (details in "Node type customization"). — [Godot docs – Import configuration](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/importing_3d_scenes/import_configuration.html)

### Inferences
- A post-import script is the right place for **mechanical** import rules (turn off LODs for meshes with a given suffix or name, assign classes from `pravidla_modelu.json`, set the visibility range), so that hand fixes in the Import dock do not disappear on re-import. This is the import-level equivalent of "no patching".
- Thin geometry (fences, grass blades, a bridge's railings) is a typical candidate for broken automatic LODs; for these, LODs off plus a visibility range.
- On Android (Mobile renderer), do not count on a soft LOD fade; use Disabled with hysteresis or a dithered material.

### Gaps
- Not checked: normal merge/split angle and mesh compression in the 4.x importer (not in the pages read); third-party "Blender to Godot" addons; splitting large scenes (scene streaming) – not reached within the call limit.

## 4. Animals and NPCs: animation cheaply and believably (Godot 4, Android)

### Takeaway
Animals without a walk clip: rather than swinging bones from a script, use **SkeletonModifier3D** (it runs after AnimationMixer, which suits a procedural layer). Step targets plus two-bone IK (TwoBoneIK3D in 4.6+) and **speed tied to stride length** prevent sliding feet. For performance, Godot **does not cull skeletons automatically**: AnimationPlayer/Tree must be turned off off-screen and at distance, and distant ones updated less often (callback mode Manual + `advance()`). Herds can use VAT on a MultiMesh.

### Cited Findings
- **SkeletonModifier3D:** a child of Skeleton3D, intended for "custom IK solvers, constraints, or skeleton physics". The override is `_process_modification_with_delta(delta)` (the older `_process_modification()` is deprecated). `influence` is blended by Skeleton3D automatically, so the modifier writes the full result. The modification **always runs after AnimationMixer**. Subclasses include LookAtModifier3D, IKModifier3D, SpringBoneSimulator3D, BoneConstraint3D, and SkeletonIK3D (deprecated). — [Godot docs – SkeletonModifier3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_skeletonmodifier3d.html)
- SkeletonIK3D is deprecated; the recommendation is SkeletonModifier3D with your own IK or a third-party solution. — [Godot docs – SkeletonIK3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.2/classes/class_skeletonik3d.html)
- Godot 4.6 brings IK back through IKModifier3D with TwoBoneIK3D for foot planting (a single secondary source; check against the 4.6 docs). — [TabNews](https://www.tabnews.com.br/zivaengine/godot-4-6-trouxe-o-ik-de-volta-pra-quem-faz-jogo-3d-isso-importa-mais-que-o-jolt); the SkeletonModifier3D page (4.7) lists IKModifier3D among its subclasses, which supports this. — [Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_skeletonmodifier3d.html)
- **Procedural steps (spider, Godot 4.5):** an IK target makes an arc-shaped step when the foot moves further than a threshold from its ideal position; legs step in diagonal pairs (only one group in the air at a time); body height = average of the feet. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/ik-driven-procedural-spider-locomotion-in-godot-4-5)
- **Sliding feet:** scale playback speed to the actual movement speed (if the clip covers 5 m/s at 1×, then 7 m/s means 1.4×). — [GameDev StackExchange](https://backiee.wasmer.app/http_gamedev_stackexchange_com/a/7262); stride warping scales leg reach by the ratio of actual speed to the clip's speed, with a minimum threshold when starting and stopping. — [Unreal StrideWarping](https://docs.unrealengine.com/PythonAPI/class/AnimNode_StrideWarping.html); SIGGRAPH 2021: shorten or lengthen the stride while the planted foot stays fixed, and lower the step height for shorter strides. — [Heuvel, The Right Foot in the Wrong Place](https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/2021-Talks-Heuvel_The-Right-Foot-in-the-Wrong-Place.pdf)
- A very slowed walk looks worse than a shortened stride; foot lift must be lowered too, otherwise it looks like "high-stepping". — [Autodesk Crowd Stride Control](https://download.autodesk.com/global/docs/softimage2014/en_us/files/iceref_Crowd_Stride_Control.htm)
- **Animation LOD in Godot:** AnimationMixer `callback_mode_process` = Physics/Idle/**Manual**; in Manual mode the animation is driven by calling `advance(delta)`, so animals far away can be updated, for example, every 4th frame with an accumulated delta. — [Godot docs – AnimationMixer](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.2/classes/class_animationmixer.html)
- Godot does not cull skeletons by itself; a 4.5 user turned off the AnimationPlayer for off-screen/distant units and reached 200+ units. Another dev fixed lag by removing AnimationTrees from skeleton objects. Reported numbers: 100 characters at 75 FPS on Compatibility vs 500+ on Mobile/Forward+ (anecdotal). — [Godot forum – crowds](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/performance-of-crowds-of-animated-rigged-characters-in-3d/130409), [Godot forum – skeletons lagging](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/skeletons-lagging-game-even-not-actively-simulated/103690), [r/godot](https://lr.sudovanilla.org/r/godot/comments/1fivfwd/animation_tree_killing_fps)
- VisibleOnScreenEnabler3D/Notifier3D is a rough heuristic (ignores occlusion), good enough to turn off animation off-screen. — [Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.1/classes/class_visibleonscreenenabler3d.html)
- **VAT (vertex animation textures) for Godot 4:** OpenVAT-for-Godot (an `OpenVATMultiMeshInstance3D` node, instanced animation, automatic configuration on import) — [Godot forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/openvat-for-godot-animated-multimeshinstance3d-using-vertex-animation-textures/139440), [GitHub antzGames/OpenVAT-for-Godot](https://github.com/antzGames/OpenVAT-for-Godot); Godot_VAT3 (a port of SideFX Labs VAT shaders) — [SideFX forum](https://www.sidefx.com/forum/post/394972), [GitHub G4ND44/Godot_VAT3](https://github.com/G4ND44/Godot_VAT3). Mobile performance is not documented by either.
- **CC0 animals:** Quaternius "Ultimate Animated Animals" – 12 animals, "more than 12" animations (Attack, Death, Gallop, Walk, Jump…), FBX/OBJ/Blend/**glTF**, CC0 (the page does not name the animals). — [quaternius.com](https://quaternius.com/packs/ultimateanimatedanimals.html); "LowPoly Animated Animals" – 6 animals, Death/Idle/Jump/Run/Walk, FBX/OBJ/Blend, CC0, tags cow, horse, llama, pig, pug. — [itch.io](https://quaternius.itch.io/lowpoly-animated-animals); the older "LowPoly Animated Farm Animal Pack" (2018, CC0, tags include sheep, pig, cow, horse, dog). — [OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/lowpoly-animated-farm-animal-pack)

### Inferences
- Recommended approach for our chicken, sheep and pig (Sketchfab rigs without walk clips): (1) first look for a CC0 replacement that has Walk/Idle (Quaternius Ultimate: check whether it contains a sheep, pig and chicken; the farm pack has a sheep and pig); (2) if we keep the Sketchfab models, replace the bone swinging with a SkeletonModifier3D node: for each leg a foot target on the ground (a raycast down, or terrain height from data), a step when the distance exceeds the threshold, diagonal pairs for quadrupeds (left front + right back), an arc of height h, and two-bone IK (TwoBoneIK3D if we are on 4.6+). That gives planted feet with no sliding automatically.
- If we stay with swinging (cheaper): tie the phase to distance travelled, not time: `phase += speed*delta / stride_length`. At speed 0 the phase stops (no "walking on the spot"), and amplitude and lift shrink proportionally at low speed (Autodesk).
- Animation LOD for animals in the village: <15 m every frame; 15–40 m Manual + `advance()` every 2nd–4th frame; >40 m or off-screen: AnimationPlayer `active=false` and the modifier `active=false` (pose frozen), possibly just a visibility range. The numbers are a proposal (NOT VERIFIED on the tablet).
- Herds of 2–10 animals in a medieval village do not need VAT; VAT only makes sense for dozens or more (flocks, crowds). For a few animals, skeletons with the LOD above are simpler.
- Overlap (animals walking into each other): simple boids separation – the sum of vectors away from neighbours within radius r, weighted by 1/d, added to the steering. Neighbours from a simple grid. Spawn points should go through the same placement and zone rules as other world parts (not in water, not on a building) – that ties into the world test.
